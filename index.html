📝 Teacher Notes & Reminders
  • Typing Jungle is now a floor, not a ceiling: students who reach the lesson floor early move to Dynamic Lessons (adaptive drill on their own weakest keys) instead of idling or racing ahead into unrelated content.
  • Stands and keyboards live at the front of both rooms. Push students toward independence (grabbing their own) by Rotation 4.
  • CodeCombat teacher dashboard lets you monitor student progress in real time — glance at the class view during coding blocks.
  • WPM goals are targets, not cutoffs — and now carry a paired accuracy goal. Some students will exceed them; celebrate that. Some will trail; encourage consistency over speed.
  • Check-in variety: 18 rotating formats mean no student repeats a format twice in a row — see the Check-in Formats Glossary above for what each one asks students to do.
  • Women in STEM presentations use the PVLEGS rubric. Remind students during typing and coding time that presentations are coming up.
  • Grade Tracker: collect the Canvas sharing link as an editor around the midpoint of the quarter so you only need one link for the whole quarter.
  • Co-teacher: touch base before each rotation. Filler time in Rotation 1 can vary a lot by period — have a backup plan ready.
  • CS1→GD1 handoff: the Arena is intentionally hard with catch-up rotations before the graded attempt. GD1 levels run slower on purpose — let students play with their creations.

🚀 CS2 Pacing Notes (CodeCombat)
  • Levels 1–7 move at a reasonable clip — most students keep pace comfortably.
  • Level 8 onward: expect about 1 level per rotation for most students. That's normal — do not rush this section.
  • Fast finishers should work premium challenge levels rather than just racing ahead — the CS2 concepts need time to sink in.
  • R6 has no CodeCombat — that's the Code.org Pixelation (Binary → RGB → Hex) activity instead. CodeCombat resumes R7.
  • R19–R20 are a Bridge to AI + handoff to the Q3 AI HackStack — no new CodeCombat levels, just wrap-up and reflection.
📝 Teacher Notes & Reminders — Q2
  • Cricut overflow from Q1: have students email you to schedule IS make-up time rather than holding the whole class back.
  • Binary to RGB connection: the Pixelation activity in R6 is a great bridge — binary becomes visual and suddenly makes sense. Lean into the "aha moment."
  • Numbers begin mid-quarter (R13): expect a visible WPM dip when numbers start — that's expected, not a regression. Number Row Boss check-in exists specifically to normalize it.
  • Start-Up Challenge buy-in is everything — if students are excited about their product idea, the rest flows. Spend real time on the brainstorm.
  • Co-teacher coordination: lab days require split scheduling for some periods. Set a timer and be strict about the swap.
  • Grade Tracker: remind students to update their Q2 sheet mid-quarter so you can access it for grading.
  • Typing form: the 10-pt deduction for incorrect form applies from the first graded test — announce it clearly so students aren't surprised.
  • Code.org Pixelation: preview the lesson yourself before R6 so you can guide students who get stuck on the RGB/Hex toggle.
  • Presentations: print the PVLEGS rubric for each presenter ahead of time to streamline scoring.

🤖 AI HackStack — The Prompt Budget Constraint
  • 10 AI prompts per student per week, 100 per teacher per month. At ~2.3 rotations/week that's roughly 4 prompts per student per rotation — the single biggest design constraint on the hackathon.
  • Draft on paper first. Students draft and refine prompts before spending one — this forces intentional prompting instead of slot-machine guessing, which is the actual skill they should leave with.
  • Teacher budget: use your 100 monthly prompts for demos only — about 25 a week.
  • Before Q3 starts: confirm your license tier includes AI 1, AI 2, Career Readiness 1–2, Algebra + AI, Cybersecurity + AI, and AI Explorations. Student licenses are required for all of them.

Judging categories: Most Useful, Most Creative, Best Prompt Craft, Best Teamwork — so more teams win something, and Best Prompt Craft rewards the thinking rather than the polish. Sprint 0 has no AI access on purpose: problem statement, paper prototype, written prompt budget before anyone touches a model.

📝 Teacher Notes & Reminders — Q3
  • Symbols begin R9 — expect another visible WPM dip. Tell students it's coming or they'll think they're regressing.
  • Feature freeze at R15 — no new hackathon features after this point; remaining time is polish, rehearsal, and demo prep only.
  • Demo Day (R18) is graded using the PVLEGS rubric — print copies ahead of time.
