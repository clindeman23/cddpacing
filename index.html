📝 Teacher Notes & Reminders
  • Typing Jungle locks: remember to unlock lesson batches in the teacher dashboard — students cannot progress past a locked lesson.
  • Stands and keyboards live at the front of both rooms. Push students toward independence (grabbing their own) by Rotation 4.
  • CodeCombat teacher dashboard lets you monitor student progress in real time — glance at the class view during coding blocks.
  • WPM goals are targets, not cutoffs. Some students will exceed them; celebrate that. Some will trail; encourage consistency over speed.
  • Check-in screenshots can be submitted as image uploads or Google Slide inserts on Canvas — whatever is simplest for your class.
  • Women in STEM presentations use the PVLEGS rubric. Remind students during typing and coding time that presentations are coming up.
  • Grade Tracker: collect the Canvas sharing link as an editor in Rotation 12–13 so you only need one link for the whole quarter.
  • Co-teacher: touch base before each rotation. Filler time in Rotation 1 can vary a lot by period — have a backup plan ready.
  • CS1→GD1 handoff: the Arena (R10–12) is intentionally hard with two catch-up rotations before the graded attempt in R12. GD1 levels (R13+) run slower on purpose — let students play with their creations.

🚀 CS2 Pacing Notes (CodeCombat)
  • Levels 1–7 move at a reasonable clip — most students keep pace comfortably.
  • Level 8 onward: expect about 1 level per rotation for most students. That's normal — do not rush this section.
  • Fast finishers should work premium challenge levels rather than just racing ahead — the CS2 concepts need time to sink in.
  • R6 has no CodeCombat — that's the Code.org Pixelation (Binary → RGB → Hex) activity instead. CodeCombat resumes R7.
  • R19–R20 are a Bridge to AI + handoff to the Q3 AI Hackathon — no new CodeCombat levels, just wrap-up and reflection.
📝 Teacher Notes & Reminders — Q2
  • Cricut overflow from Q1: have students email you to schedule IS make-up time rather than holding the whole class back.
  • Binary to RGB connection: the Pixelation activity in R6 is a great bridge — binary becomes visual and suddenly makes sense. Lean into the "aha moment."
  • Lesson pacing update: Q2's lesson ceiling is now 240 (was 274+ in the original draft) — the Typing Jungle numbers section doesn't get reached this quarter under the revised pacing, so that content shifts to Q3.
  • Start-Up Challenge buy-in is everything — if students are excited about their product idea, the rest flows. Spend real time on the brainstorm in R9.
  • Co-teacher coordination: R4–R5 lab days require split scheduling for some periods. Set a timer and be strict about the 35-min swap.
  • Grade Tracker: remind students to update their Q2 sheet around R6 so you can access it mid-quarter for grading.
  • Typing form: the 10-pt deduction for incorrect form is new this quarter — announce it clearly in R5 so students aren't surprised.
  • Code.org Pixelation: preview the lesson yourself before R6 so you can guide students who get stuck on the RGB/Hex toggle.
  • Presentations (R19–R20): print the PVLEGS rubric for each presenter ahead of time to streamline scoring.
